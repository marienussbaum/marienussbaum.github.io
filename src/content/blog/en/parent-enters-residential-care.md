The admission of a parent into a nursing home for dependent older adults can act as a catalyst within family dynamics. Relationships between siblings, often long‑established, may be reassessed and transformed in this context. Caregiving responsibilities, emotional management, and decision‑making can intensify existing tensions or generate new ones. This period may strengthen sibling bonds, but it can also revive old resentments.

For therapists, it is essential to pay close attention to this family dimension in order to grasp the subtleties of interactions and potential conflicts.

Aging is a natural process, yet it is often accompanied by a loss of autonomy and the need for appropriate care. When remaining at home is no longer possible due to the parent’s health condition, moving into a nursing home becomes a relevant option. However, this transition represents an emotional upheaval for families, filled with ambivalent feelings such as guilt, sadness, and sometimes even relief. The decision to place a parent in a nursing home is often a source of guilt. The therapist’s role is to help clarify the underlying motivations, assess possible alternatives, and support the family in accepting the decision with compassion.

In this context, psychological support plays a key role. We intervene to frame, support, and guide our patients throughout this complex process. Several main areas of intervention emerge. We begin by assessing the specific situation of each family. It is crucial to provide clear and empathetic information about the reasons that make nursing home placement necessary.

The emotions involved are often intense and contradictory. Creating a safe space where adult children can express their feelings freely, without fear of judgment, is essential. Validating these emotions is the first step toward easing them.The parent’s departure to an institution can stir deep anxieties, particularly the fear of no longer recognizing one’s own parent. The possibility of witnessing the parent’s identity shift or fade can be frightening. This transition also often leads adult children to confront their own aging and to imagine what the future may hold for them.

It may also be an opportunity to revisit or take stock of the parent‑child relationship.

The therapist’s task is also to support the family members in what they may wish to address with the parent entering the facility, helping them build a meaningful relationship during this new stage of life.
